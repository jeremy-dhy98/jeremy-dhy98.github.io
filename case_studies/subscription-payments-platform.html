Case study

Subscription & M-Pesa Payments Platform

A Flask backend that lets customers pay by M-Pesa and then confirms the payment, activates access, emails the customer and records everything automatically.

  1. M-Pesa checkout
  2. IntaSend
  3. Flask webhook
  4. Access, email & records

The problem

Selling access to premium content to Kenyan customers needs local payments. Done manually, every payment means checking a statement, switching on access and updating a record by hand.

The solution

Customers pay through an M-Pesa prompt on their phone, processed by IntaSend. The Flask backend receives IntaSend’s payment webhooks, confirms the payment, activates the subscription, sends the customer an email and writes the record to Google Sheets. No one reconciles anything by hand.

Architecture

  • Checkout: M-Pesa prompt triggered through IntaSend.
  • Confirmation: webhook callbacks from IntaSend, backed up by live status streaming to the browser and direct status checks, so a missed or late callback does not leave a customer waiting.
  • Records: Google Sheets as the subscriber database, fronted by a short-lived cache.
  • Delivery: automatic emails over SMTP.

Technologies

Flask, IntaSend, M-Pesa, webhooks, Google Sheets API, SMTP email, Server-Sent Events.

Lessons learned

  • Never rely on one confirmation channel. Webhooks can arrive late or not at all, so the system also checks payment status directly.
  • Spreadsheet APIs have rate limits. Polling during checkout would exhaust them, so user records are cached briefly and refreshed after every write.
  • Payment states are ambiguous. A generic “processing” state does not tell you whether the money came from the customer’s balance or from an overdraft, so the interface now uses neutral wording and reacts the moment a payment is declined or confirmed.
Result

Replaced manual payment checks and reconciliation with automatic M-Pesa confirmation, access activation and record-keeping.
